Choosing a Charger & Cutting Your Running Costs

The charger itself is only half the picture — how you power it can make a bigger difference to your bills than which box is on the wall.

7kW is the right size for almost every UK home. It runs off a standard single-phase supply, adds roughly 25–30 miles of range per hour, and fully charges a typical EV overnight. 22kW chargers need a three-phase supply, which most UK homes don't have — upgrading for one is rarely worth it unless you already have three-phase power.

Tethered vs untethered is really just a preference. A tethered charger has the cable permanently attached — quicker to use, especially in bad weather. An untethered charger is just a socket — you supply your own cable, which suits households with more than one EV or different connector types. Charging speed is identical either way.

💷 Cheap Overnight Tariffs

A smart charger lets you schedule charging for off-peak hours. EV-specific tariffs like Intelligent Octopus Go offer overnight rates that typically sit at a fraction of the standard Ofgem price cap rate — often around a third of the daytime price. Exact rates change regularly, so it's worth checking current pricing with your supplier before switching.

☀️ Solar Integration

Some smart chargers automatically divert surplus solar generation into your car instead of exporting it to the grid, topping up for free whenever the sun's out. If you already have solar panels, or you're thinking about them, it's worth asking whether a solar-diverting charger makes sense for your setup — we can advise on compatible options when we quote.

Tariff names, rates and grant terms above are correct at the time of writing and change periodically — always confirm current figures directly with your energy supplier or on gov.uk before making a decision based on them.

No Driveway? Here's How Home Charging Still Works.

Most EV charger information assumes you have off-street parking. If you park on the street in Boscombe, Westbourne, or anywhere else in BCP, there's still a route to charging at home — it's just less well explained.

The answer is a cross-pavement channel — a permanent cable gully set into the pavement that lets a charging cable run safely from your house, across the footpath, to your car parked on the street. It has to be a proper permanent installation, not a temporary cable cover or mat.

Because the work crosses public pavement, it needs approval from your local highways authority — BCP Council for Poole, Bournemouth and Christchurch — before it goes in. We handle this process for you: surveying the route, submitting the application, and only installing once it's approved.

Common Questions

I don't have a driveway — can I still get a home EV charger?

Often yes, via a cross-pavement channel — a permanent cable gully set into the pavement that lets you charge from your house across the footpath. It needs local highways authority permission, which we can help you apply for.

How much is the government grant worth?

As of April 2026, the grant covers 75% of the purchase and installation cost, up to a maximum of £500 per socket. It applies to on-street parking properties, renters and flat owners with a private space, and residential landlords, and is open until 31 March 2027.

Do I need permission from the council to install a cross-pavement channel?

Yes. Because the cable crosses public pavement, your local highways authority (BCP Council for Poole, Bournemouth and Christchurch) has to approve the installation before it goes in. Installing without approval can mean the grant is refused.

How long does installation take?

A standard driveway installation usually takes half a day. A cross-pavement installation takes longer, since it depends on council approval timing as well as the physical work.
