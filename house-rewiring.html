Full Rewire or Partial Rewire?

Not every property needs everything replacing — we'll tell you honestly which one yours actually needs.

Full Rewire

Every circuit in the property is replaced — sockets, lighting, and the consumer unit. The right choice for older properties where the whole installation is dated or unsafe.

Partial Rewire

Only specific circuits or areas are replaced — one floor, a kitchen extension, or a single problem circuit — while sound existing wiring is left in place.

Signs Your Home Might Need Rewiring

If any of these sound familiar, it's worth getting it looked at.

Round-pin sockets or a mix of old and new socket styles

A fusebox with rewireable fuses instead of modern circuit breakers

Frequent tripping or fuses that blow regularly

Warm sockets or switches, or a burning smell near an outlet

Visible fabric or rubber-insulated cable rather than modern plastic sheathing

No rewiring since before the 1980s, or an EICR that's come back Unsatisfactory

What Happens On the Day

1. First fix

New cabling is run under floors, through walls and ceilings, and back to the consumer unit. This is the dusty, disruptive part — we protect floors and furniture with dust sheets throughout.

2. Second fix

Sockets, switches, and light fittings go on, and the new consumer unit is installed and connected.

3. Testing & certification

Every circuit is tested before we hand over an Electrical Installation Certificate (EIC) confirming the work meets BS 7671.

4. Making good

We're electricians, not plasterers — we'll leave any chased walls ready for a decorator, and clear away our own mess as we go.

Building Regulations & Certification

Rewiring a home is notifiable work under Part P of the Building Regulations — meaning it legally has to be signed off, either by your local council's building control team or by an electrician registered with a competent person scheme.

We're NICEIC registered, which means we self-certify the work ourselves. You don't need to contact building control separately or pay an extra fee — we issue your Electrical Installation Certificate (EIC) as part of the job, and it's the document you'll need if you ever sell the property.

Common Questions

How do I know if my house needs rewiring?

Common signs include round-pin sockets, a fusebox with rewireable fuses instead of circuit breakers, frequent tripping, sockets that feel warm, flickering lights, or wiring you can see is wrapped in fabric or rubber rather than modern plastic. If your property hasn't been rewired since before the 1980s, it's worth having it checked.

What's the difference between a full and partial rewire?

A full rewire replaces every circuit in the property — sockets, lighting, and the consumer unit. A partial rewire only replaces specific circuits or areas, such as one floor or a kitchen extension, while leaving sound existing wiring in place.

How long does a rewire take and how disruptive is it?

A full rewire on an average 3-bedroom house typically takes 4–6 days. It's dusty work since cables run under floors and behind walls, but we use dust sheets throughout and make good the immediate area we've worked in before we leave.

Do I need permission from the council to rewire my house?

Rewiring is notifiable work under Part P of the Building Regulations, but because we're NICEIC registered, we self-certify the work ourselves. You don't need to contact building control or pay a separate fee — we handle the certification as part of the job.
