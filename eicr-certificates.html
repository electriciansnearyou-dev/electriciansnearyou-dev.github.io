What Is an EICR?

An Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) is a formal inspection of the fixed wiring in a property — the consumer unit, sockets, switches, and cabling behind the walls. It's not a check of appliances; it's a check of the electrical installation itself against the current wiring regulations (BS 7671).

A qualified engineer tests every circuit, checks earthing and bonding, and inspects the consumer unit. Anything found is logged with a classification code, and the report is marked either Satisfactory or Unsatisfactory depending on what's found.

Your Legal Duty as a Landlord

Under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020, every private landlord in England has these duties.

DutyDeadline
Have the installation inspected and testedAt least every 5 years, or sooner if the previous report specifies
Give existing tenants a copy of the reportWithin 28 days of the inspection
Give new tenants a copy of the reportBefore they move in
Complete any C1 or C2 remedial workWithin 28 days of the test date, or sooner if the report says so
Provide a copy if a local council requests oneWithin 7 days

Penalty for non-compliance: up to £40,000

Local councils can fine landlords per breach, arrange remedial work themselves and recover the cost from you, and — since 1 May 2026 — the maximum penalty was raised from £30,000 to £40,000 and the duty was extended to cover social housing too.

Understanding Your Report's Codes

If your EICR isn't a straightforward pass, it'll list findings under one of three codes. Here's what each one actually means.

C1

Danger present

A risk of injury right now. We make this safe immediately, on the spot, before we leave.

C2

Potentially dangerous

Urgent remedial action needed. This is what makes a report "Unsatisfactory" and starts your 28-day clock.

C3

Improvement recommended

Doesn't meet current standards but isn't unsafe. Noted for your awareness — no legal action required.

Common Questions

Do I need an EICR if I only just bought the property?

Yes — the duty applies to any residential tenancy, regardless of how long you've owned the property. If there's no valid, in-date EICR on file, you're not compliant.

What happens if my report comes back Unsatisfactory?

Any C1 items get made safe immediately during the visit. C2 items need fixing within 28 days — we'll quote the remedial work on the spot and can usually book it in straight away.

How long does the inspection take?

Most standard 2–3 bedroom properties take 2–3 hours. Larger properties or HMOs take longer — we'll give you a time estimate when you book.

Can you test while the property is tenanted?

Yes, that's the most common scenario. We'll need brief access to turn circuits off one at a time — we coordinate timing with your tenant directly if useful.
